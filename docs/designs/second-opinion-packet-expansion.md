## Problem Statement

Ayukiz started as a Doctor Visit Optimizer for remote adult children managing elder-parent care in India. The first approved wedge was a doctor-ready packet for one symptom episode: prescriptions, reports, medicines, allergies, missing information, and questions for the next consultation.

The scope now expands to a sharper adjacent use case: second-opinion visits where prior care is scattered, the next doctor lacks evidence, and the family is relying on memory at the point of care.

The expansion should not become a broad personal health record. The useful product is a second-opinion packet that helps the next doctor see the relevant facts quickly.

## Demand Evidence

- Elder-parent case: prior doctor visits, changing prescriptions, recurring symptoms, and family uncertainty around whether the next doctor has the full context.
- Friend case: the patient had a knee/ankle problem, had already consulted 2-3 doctors while in Paris, then moved to Bhopal and faced the problem again.
- He had two earlier prescriptions but did not carry records to the new doctor.
- The new doctor first asked about allergy, BP, and diabetes, then moved to the current issue.
- The patient answered from memory.
- The prescription included steroids. He noticed possible risk only when he was about to pay a large medicine bill, searched the medicine on ChatGPT, and realized why medical history matters.
- What he should have been able to show: earlier prescriptions, medicines prescribed, and scans.

The validated pain is not "the product prevents misdiagnosis." That claim is too clinical and unsafe for V1. The validated pain is missing evidence, medicine confusion, and expensive uncertainty before or after a new doctor visit.

## Status Quo

Patients and families carry medical context in memory, paper, WhatsApp photos, and scattered prescriptions.

For second opinions or city changes:

- Prior prescriptions may not be carried to the appointment.
- Scans or reports may exist but are not organized for the doctor.
- Allergy, BP, diabetes, hypertension, and current medicines are answered from memory.
- Medicine changes are discovered after the visit, often at the pharmacy or payment moment.
- Families use informal tools like ChatGPT to understand prescribed medicines, but without a safe clinical boundary.

The status quo fails at the transition point: a new doctor sees the patient without the full prior-care context.

## Target User And Narrowest Wedge

First buyer: the remote adult child managing elder-parent care in India.

Expanded use case: the elder parent is going for a second opinion or seeing a new doctor after 2-3 confusing or ineffective consultations.

Narrowest paid product: a Second-Opinion Doctor Packet for one elder-parent case.

The packet includes:

- Patient snapshot: age, sex, allergy status, BP/diabetes/hypertension status, chronic conditions, and current medicines.
- Current problem timeline in family language.
- Prior doctor visits and prescriptions.
- Scans and reports list, with source links or attachment labels.
- Medicine list extracted from current and prior prescriptions.
- Stop/continue ambiguity and duplicate-by-name flags.
- Questions to ask the next doctor.
- Post-visit capture fields for diagnosis, medicine changes, follow-up, and unresolved questions.

### Packet Output Outline

The V1 output is a manually assembled PDF or document that a doctor can scan in under 60 seconds.

Required sections, in order:

1. One-page patient snapshot: name or nickname, age, sex, allergy status, BP/diabetes/hypertension status, known chronic conditions, and current medicines as reported by family.
2. Current issue timeline: symptom, duration, severity stated by family, prior episodes, and planned consultation.
3. Prior care summary: each prior doctor visit, location, date if known, prescription attachment label, scans or reports, and reported outcome.
4. Medicine table: exact medicine names as extracted from documents, source prescription, dosage text if readable, confidence level, and family-confirmed status.
5. Ambiguity list: unclear handwriting, missing allergy status, old medicines not mentioned in the latest prescription, exact-name duplicate medicines, and missing stop/continue instructions.
6. Questions for the next doctor: factual questions only, phrased for clinician decision.
7. Post-visit feedback fields: what the doctor said, medicine changes, follow-up date, and whether the packet changed the consultation.

Optional sections:

- Scan/report index.
- Family concerns in their own words.
- Pharmacist or clinician reviewer note, only when a named reviewer actually reviewed the packet.

## Constraints

- Ayukiz must not claim to diagnose, prevent misdiagnosis, decide treatment, or approve/reject prescribed medicines.
- Without pharmacist or clinician review, medicine work remains extraction, exact-name duplicate flagging, ambiguity flagging, and question generation.
- Real patient records must stay outside git in restricted storage.
- The pilot should remain manually operated until 5-10 real packets prove repeat demand.
- Patient history is evidence for the visit packet, not a standalone health wallet in V1.

### Consent And Pilot Data Handling

Consent requirement:

- If the elder parent can consent, capture written WhatsApp consent or a short recorded confirmation before processing documents.
- If the adult child uploads on behalf of the parent, record that the adult child confirms they have permission to share the records for packet preparation.
- If consent is missing or unclear, stop packet preparation and request consent before reviewing documents.

Pilot storage rule:

- Store real prescriptions, scans, reports, completed packets, consent records, and reviewer notes in a restricted non-git folder.
- No real patient files, completed packets, WhatsApp screenshots, or reviewer notes belong in the repo.
- Limit access to the founder/operator and any named reviewer assigned to that case.
- Retain pilot files for 30 days after packet delivery unless the family explicitly asks to keep the case active.
- Provide deletion over the same intake channel: the family can ask for deletion by WhatsApp or form response, and the operator must remove the case folder.
- Do not reuse real prescriptions, scans, reports, case details, packets, or reviewer notes for demos, training, examples, marketing, or synthetic test cases without explicit separate permission.

The actual storage tool remains a blocker from `TODOS.md`. Until it is chosen, do not accept real non-family pilot records.

### Safety Modes

V1 has two operating modes.

No clinical reviewer available:

- Allowed: organize documents, extract exact text, mark unclear fields, list exact-name duplicate medicine entries, identify missing allergy/BP/diabetes/hypertension status, and generate questions for the doctor.
- Not allowed: mention drug interactions, say a medicine is risky for a condition, recommend stopping or starting medicine, interpret scans or reports, or judge urgency beyond showing conservative red-flag language.

Reviewer available:

- Pharmacist reviewer can address medicine interaction, duplicate therapy, dosage concern, chronic-condition medicine risk, or medicine-cost alternatives.
- Doctor or nurse reviewer can address symptom escalation, care-setting questions, clinical interpretation, or urgency.
- Every reviewer note must include reviewer name, role, review date, and what was reviewed.

### Red-Flag Boundary

Ayukiz does not triage emergencies. It only uses conservative routing language.

If the family reports severe pain, chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, confusion, blood in vomit or stool, high fever, repeated vomiting, rapidly worsening symptoms, or any emergency concern, the packet flow shows:

"This may need urgent medical attention. Ayukiz cannot decide emergency severity. Please contact your doctor, local emergency service, or nearest hospital now. We can organize documents only if doing so does not delay care."

The product must never downgrade urgency or say it is safe to wait.

## Premises

1. The first expanded user is still the remote adult child managing elder-parent care, not every patient seeking a second opinion.
2. The expanded product is a Second-Opinion Doctor Packet, not a full personal health record.
3. The safer promise is: reduce missing-history mistakes, medicine confusion, and wasted consultation time before the next doctor visit.
4. Patient history is the reusable substrate that makes packets faster and more evidence-backed after repeat cases.
5. The narrowest paid pilot remains manual: run 5-10 second-opinion packets before building login, permanent profiles, automatic upload infrastructure, or a broad health wallet.

## Landscape Notes

Broad personal health records have adoption problems: patient effort, health literacy, provider trust, workflow fit, privacy, and continued-use burden.

Medication and history records matter most at care transitions: new doctor, new hospital, discharge, second opinion, and medicine changes. That supports an appointment-triggered packet rather than an always-maintained record app.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Second-Opinion Packet Extension

Expand the existing Doctor Visit Optimizer into a second-opinion packet for elder-parent cases. Add prior-doctor context, old prescriptions, scans, chronic-condition flags, current medicines, allergy/BP/diabetes/hypertension status, and "what changed between doctors?" questions.

Why chosen: it absorbs the new evidence without breaking the existing wedge. It keeps the buyer narrow and can be piloted manually this week.

### Approach B: Evidence-Backed Patient History Profile

Create a reusable patient profile for allergies, chronic conditions, medicines, prescriptions, scans, and visit history.

Why not first: it is a better long-term substrate, but too broad before proof. Families may not maintain it until a visit creates urgency.

### Approach C: Post-Prescription Medicine Question Check

After the visit, let the family upload the new prescription before buying medicine. Flag expensive medicines, duplicate-by-name issues, unclear stop/continue instructions, and questions for a doctor or pharmacist.

Why not first: this is sharp but enters medication-risk territory quickly. It should come after a pharmacist reviewer is available.

## Recommended Approach

Build the Second-Opinion Packet Extension.

The product promise should be:

"Walk into the next doctor visit with the history, prescriptions, scans, medicines, and questions that matter."

Do not use:

"Avoid misdiagnosis."

### Data Additions To Existing Case Schema

Add only fields needed for the manual template and future eval format. These extend the existing shared case shape; they are not product database fields yet:

- Existing `symptom_episode` gains `visit_type`: first_visit | follow_up | second_opinion | new_city_or_hospital.
- Existing `symptom_episode` gains `prior_consultations_count` and `prior_locations`.
- Existing `patient.known_conditions` is paired with `patient.condition_screening`: allergy_status, bp_history, diabetes_status, hypertension_status.
- Existing `documents` can reference prior prescriptions, scans, and reports with `prior_care_ref`.
- Existing `review` can record second-opinion reviewer need, reviewer availability, and unresolved clinical questions.
- Existing `family_questions` becomes the source for second-opinion doctor and medicine questions.

Do not add permanent account, family graph, health wallet, insurance, hospital integration, or medication-risk scoring in this scope.

### Medicine Ambiguity Rules

Safe V1 flags:

- Exact-name duplicate: the same medicine name appears in more than one prescription or in family-entered current medicines.
- Stop/continue ambiguity: an older prescription includes a medicine, the newer prescription does not mention whether to stop it, and the family says they are unsure.
- Unclear dosage: the dosage or frequency is unreadable or missing.
- Missing condition screen: allergy, BP, diabetes, or hypertension status is unknown.

Unsafe without reviewer:

- Same-generic detection across brands.
- Drug-drug interaction claims.
- Steroid-risk claims for diabetes or other conditions.
- Dose correctness.
- Whether a prescription is appropriate.

Safe wording:

"This item needs doctor/pharmacist clarification."

Unsafe wording:

"Do not take this medicine" or "this medicine is unsafe for you."

### Manual Operations SLA

Pilot target:

- Standard case: 30-60 minutes of operator work after complete intake.
- Complex case: mark as best effort when there are more than 8 photos, more than 20 pages, unclear handwriting, missing dates, or multilingual documents the operator cannot confidently read.
- Appointment cutoff: if the consultation is less than 2 hours away, provide only a compressed packet: patient snapshot, latest prescription, top missing facts, and top doctor questions.
- Unclear fields are allowed. Guessing is not allowed.

## Success Criteria

First 10 second-opinion pilots should answer:

- At least 5 families complete a second-opinion packet before a real consultation.
- At least 3 families bring prior prescriptions or scans they otherwise would not have carried.
- At least 3 families say the packet changed what they asked the doctor.
- At least 2 families pay Rs 199-299 during pilot or agree they would pay Rs 299-799 for the next packet.
- At least 1 family returns for a later issue, proving the patient-history substrate matters.
- Zero packets include unreviewed clinical advice, diagnosis, or medicine stop/start instructions.
